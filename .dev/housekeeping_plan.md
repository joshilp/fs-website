# Motel Housekeeping & Laundry Plan

**Property:** 64-unit beach motel, up to 3 floors, no elevators, outdoor walkways, one centralized laundry room (~200 ft from furthest rooms)
**Staff:** Owner + mom + up to 2 others
**Machines:** Large commercial front-load + smaller top-load, 2 heavy-duty dryers. ~30 min wash, ~30 min dry
**Current linen:** Less than 2 par (ordering more). All queen beds. All white sheets.
**Budget:** ~$2,000

---

## Part 1: Switch to White Duvets

### Why This Makes Sense for You

Your current layering — mattress protector → flat sheet → flat sheet → brown blanket → floral cover — is slow to make, hard to wash, and looks dated. White duvets are standard in hotels because:

- One duvet cover replaces the blanket + floral cover entirely
- White can be bleached, so stains don't ruin them
- Beds are faster to make (fewer layers)
- Looks cleaner and more professional to guests

### New Bed Setup (Per Bed)

| Layer | Item | Washed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mattress protector | Monthly or on stain |
| 2 | Flat sheet (tucked over mattress) | Every turnover |
| 3 | Flat sheet (top sheet for guest) | Every turnover |
| 4 | Duvet with white duvet cover | Cover every turnover, insert monthly or on stain |

This is actually the same number of sheets you already use — you're just replacing the blanket + floral cover with a duvet + cover. Bed-making time stays the same or gets slightly faster.

### What to Buy

**Duvet inserts:**
- Get **polyester/microfiber fill** (NOT down). It's cheaper, machine washable in your front-load, dries faster, and can handle hot water and bleach.
- Aim for **1.5 par** on inserts (you won't wash every turnover)
- 64 units × 1.5 = ~96 inserts (more if multi-bed rooms)
- Bulk pricing from suppliers like Monarch Brands, Standard Textile, or Amazon Business: ~$12–20 each

**Duvet covers:**
- Get **100% white cotton or 60/40 cotton-poly**. Poly-cotton is easier to wash, dries faster, and wrinkles less.
- Aim for **2 par** on covers (washed every turnover like sheets)
- 64 units × 2 = ~128 covers (more for multi-bed rooms)
- Bulk pricing: ~$10–18 each

**Phase the purchase in if budget is tight:**
- Start with one building or floor as a pilot
- Replace floral covers first — the first two flat sheets stay the same, you just add a duvet cover on top
- Retire the brown blankets as you add inserts

### Washing Duvet Covers

- Wash with sheets — same cycle, same temperature
- Hot water (60°C / 140°F) + oxygen bleach (OxiClean or similar) for whites
- Do NOT use chlorine bleach regularly — it degrades fabric over time
- Dry fully on high heat (kills bacteria, especially important for beach property)

### Washing Duvet Inserts (Monthly or on Staining)

- Use the **large front-load washer only** — top-load agitators can damage the fill
- Warm water (not hot — hot can clump fill)
- Mild detergent, no fabric softener
- **Put 2–3 clean tennis balls in the dryer** with the insert — this redistributes the fill and prevents clumping
- Dry on medium heat, run a second cycle if needed — inserts must be completely dry or fill molds
- Check: squeeze the insert when dry. If you feel any cool/damp spots, run again.

### Dealing With Sand in Duvets

- Shake out at the door of the room before stripping — shake the duvet cover off the insert outside
- If insert is sandy, spot-vacuum or shake well before washing
- A beach-specific tip: put a **"No duvets on the beach" notice** on the in-room card if guests are known to drag them outside

## Part 2: Laundry Workflow

### The Core Problem

Right now the laundry room is reactive — you bring everything in, dump it, then figure out what to wash. The machines sit idle between loads. You're constantly waiting on clean linens mid-cleaning.

### The Fix: Start Laundry First, Before Any Rooms

**Morning sequence:**

1. First person in starts **both washers immediately** before any rooms are touched
2. You (stripping beds) feed the pipeline — but the machines should never be idle waiting for you
3. As soon as a dryer is free, load it and start the next wash
4. Goal: machines run **back-to-back all day** with no gaps

### Load Sizes (Based on Your Setup)

| Washer | Load |
|---|---|
| Large front-load (~60 lb) | 3 pillowcase-stuffed bundles of towels/pillowcases OR 3–4 duvet covers |
| Smaller top-load | 5 flat sheets OR pillowcases |
| Dryers | Both hold everything from both washers — run simultaneously |

**Cycle math for a busy day:**
- 30 min wash + 30 min dry = 60 min per round
- Both washers running = ~2 loads washing simultaneously
- If you start at 8am and run back-to-back, you can process **8–10 loads by 2pm**

### Sorting at the Source (Huge Time Saver)

Instead of dumping everything on the laundry room floor and sorting there, sort **at the room** when stripping:

- **Bag 1 (light color / mesh bag):** Towels, face cloths, hand towels, bath mats
- **Bag 2 (dark color / mesh bag):** Sheets, pillowcases, duvet covers

Use two different colored mesh laundry bags or garbage bags at each room. When you bring them to the laundry room, you load directly — no floor dumping, no sorting.

**Cost:** Mesh laundry bags, ~$20–40 for a set

### Laundry Room Organization

Even with limited space, set up these dedicated zones:

```
[ DIRTY SIDE ]          [ CLEAN SIDE ]
Incoming bags go here   Shelves labeled:
Don't let dirty touch   - Queen Flat Sheets
clean linen             - Pillowcases
                        - Bath Towels
[ MACHINES ]            - Hand Towels
Washer 1 | Washer 2     - Face Cloths / Bath Mats
Dryer 1  | Dryer 2      - Duvet Covers
                        
[ FOLDING TABLE ]       [ ROOM KITS (see below) ]
```

Buy 1–2 wire shelving units if you don't have them (~$40–80 each, like the standard chrome wire racks). Label every shelf.

### The Room Kit System

**This is the biggest time saver for bed-making.**

After laundry is folded, bundle everything needed for one bed into a single "kit":

**Per bed kit:**
- 2 flat sheets
- 1 duvet cover
- 2 pillowcases

Fold them together and stack by kit. When loading up to go clean, grab one kit per bed — no counting, no grabbing individual items.

For towels, pre-count a "room set" per bathroom:
- 2 bath towels
- 2 hand towels
- 2 face cloths
- 1 bath mat

Rubber-band or fold them together or stack them as a set.

## Part 3: Room Cleaning Workflow

### Current Problem

Staff carry vacuums, buckets of towels, soaps, cleaning supplies, and sheets room-to-room by hand. When sheets run low, someone walks back to laundry (~200 ft). This constant back-and-forth is where most time is wasted.

### The Staging System (No Expensive Carts Needed)

Since you're doing buildings one at a time, use a **staged supply drop** approach:

**What to buy:**
- 2–3 large rolling laundry bins (the kind used in commercial laundry — like a canvas hamper on wheels): ~$50–80 each
- OR large plastic rolling storage bins / wheeled trash cans (cheaper, ~$30–50 each)

**How it works:**

1. At the start of each building, load a rolling bin with:
   - Enough room kits for every room in that building
   - Towel sets for every room
   - Cleaning supplies and soaps for that building
   - Vacuum (if you only have one, it follows the cleaner)

2. Park the bin at the **ground floor entrance** of the building

3. Staff take what they need for their floor up the stairs in a small carry bag or bucket (one trip per floor)

4. Strip all rooms, leaving dirty bags outside the doors or at the stairwell

5. Make all beds (supplies already on floor, carried up once)

6. Clean bathrooms/kitchens

7. Move to next floor

8. One person collects all dirty bags from that building and takes them to laundry on the way between buildings

**Why this works:** You're not doing dozens of small trips — you're doing one big trip per building, and the dirty return trip serves double duty (you'd be walking that way anyway).

### Cleaning Sequence Per Room (Checkout ~30 min)

This order minimizes walking inside the room:

1. Open windows/door — let it air
2. Strip bed(s) — stuff into dirty bag by type (sheets vs towels)
3. Empty garbage, collect cups/dishes
4. Start bathroom wipe-down (spray surfaces to let cleaner sit)
5. Make bed(s) — grab kit, make bed while cleaner soaks
6. Back to bathroom — wipe down, clean toilet, mop/sweep floor
7. Kitchen — wipe counters, check dishes (wash if left dirty), restock dish towels
8. Sweep/vacuum floor
9. Wipe surfaces, check everything
10. Set out fresh towel sets
11. Final check, lock up

### Kitchen Units

Mom does kitchens — make sure she has a dedicated supply kit for kitchens that stays with her:
- Dish soap, sponge, cloth
- Multi-surface spray
- Extra dish towels (pre-counted per unit)
- Garbage bags

If dishes are left dirty by guests, budget 5–10 extra minutes for those rooms. It helps to have a note in each kitchen saying something like "Please clean your dishes before checkout — thank you!"

## Part 4: Par Level Recommendations

**Par** = one complete set for all rooms that need it on a given day.

Right now you have less than 2 par. You need at minimum **2 par to operate without stress** on a normal day, and **3 par for peak weekends**.

### Why 3 Par for Peak Weekends?

On a busy long weekend:
- Set 1 is on the beds in the rooms
- Set 2 is what your staff are cleaning rooms with today
- Set 3 is what you're washing/drying so Set 2 is ready for tomorrow

Without Set 3, you're racing the laundry and staff are waiting on machines mid-day.

### Suggested Inventory Targets

| Item | 2 Par (normal) | 3 Par (peak) |
|---|---|---|
| Queen flat sheets | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Pillowcases | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Duvet covers (once switched) | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Duvet inserts | ~96 | ~128 |
| Bath towels | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Hand towels | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Face cloths | ~130+ | ~195+ |
| Bath mats | ~65+ | ~100+ |
| Kitchen dish towels | ~65+ | ~100+ |

These are rough estimates — adjust based on your actual room count that have kitchens, multi-bed units, etc.

### Managing Damaged Linens

You currently keep bad ones in circulation too long. Fix this:

- Keep a small bin labeled **"RAG BIN"** in the laundry room
- Any linen with permanent staining, tears, or thinning goes in immediately when spotted
- Rags get used for cleaning (mopping, wiping outdoor furniture, etc.) — nothing wasted
- Once a month, count your inventory and order replacements

## Part 5: Peak Weekend Strategy

When you're near 100% occupancy (July/August long weekends):

### The Night Before

- Do a full laundry run the evening before a peak checkout day
- Have all linens clean, folded, and ready in room kits before your staff arrive in the morning
- This means the first load of the day starts going through the machines around 8am from the morning's stripped rooms — not from an empty pile

### Staffing Split on Peak Days

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| You | Strip rooms, load laundry, keep machines running all day |
| Mom | Kitchens + any dishes left behind |
| Staff 1 | Bed-making and bathroom cleaning — one floor |
| Staff 2 | Bed-making and bathroom cleaning — another floor |

The key insight: **laundry and room cleaning happen simultaneously all day**, not sequentially. You're not waiting for laundry before cleaning rooms — you start with last night's pre-done supply and replenish throughout the day.

### Building Priority Order

On peak days, start with the buildings furthest from laundry first — that way by the time you're working rooms closest to laundry, supply runs are shorter and you're winding down.

## Part 7: Washing Cheat Sheet (Post in Laundry Room)

```
SHEETS & DUVET COVERS
- Hot water
- Regular detergent + scoop of OxiClean
- Dry on high until fully dry
- Fold immediately or they wrinkle

TOWELS & BATH MATS
- Hot water
- Regular detergent (NO fabric softener — kills absorbency)
- Dry on high
- Fold into standard sets: 2 bath + 2 hand + 2 face + 1 mat

DUVET INSERTS (monthly or stained)
- Front-load washer only
- Warm water, mild detergent
- Add 3 tennis balls to dryer, medium heat
- Run dryer twice if needed — must be 100% dry

KITCHEN DISH TOWELS
- Wash with towels
- Check for grease stains — pre-treat with dish soap before washing

DAMAGED LINENS → RAG BIN immediately
```
